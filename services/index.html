Why it matters

What two licenses actually change.

"Licensed and insured" is on every contractor's truck. The specific licenses are what determine whether your project can legally run under one contract.

CGC#1533136 — Certified General Contractor
A Florida CGC can build, remodel and repair structures of unlimited height, including the structural work most licenses stop short of — foundations, load-bearing changes, second stories. It is the license that lets an addition be designed and built rather than assembled around what a specialty trade is allowed to touch.
CBC#1266054 — Certified Building Contractor
Covers commercial and residential structures up to three stories, alongside the general license. Holding both widens what can be pulled under one permit and one contract of record.
What that means on your job
No handoffs mid-project. No "that's not our scope" when the tile crew finds rot behind the shower. No three-way blame between a roofer, a plumber and a framer when a schedule slips — because there is one contract, one schedule and one person answering the phone.
Who pulls the permit
We do, as the contractor of record, in the correct jurisdiction for your address — and we carry the inspections through to final. Be cautious of any contractor who asks a homeowner to pull the permit on a project of real size; it quietly transfers the liability to you.

Questions

Straight answers.

Do I need separate contractors for the roof and the remodel?

Not with us, and that is the whole point of holding both licenses. A re-roof tied into an addition, or concrete poured alongside a structural change, stays on one contract and one schedule instead of splitting across trades that each blame the other for the delay.

What does a free estimate actually include?

An on-site walkthrough and a written, itemized scope — line items rather than a single round number — so you can see what is included before anything is signed. We respond within one business day.

Do you handle small jobs, or only full projects?

Both. A single bathroom, a roof repair or a driveway pour is worth doing properly. The licenses matter more on large structural work, but the same written scope and the same crew apply either way.

How far out are you booking?

It moves with the season and with storm activity. Call and we will tell you honestly — including if we are not the right fit for your timeline.

Who is actually on my job site?

Our own crews, with Michael Greig overseeing the project personally from first call to final walkthrough. We do not hand your project to a rotating cast of subs you have never met.
